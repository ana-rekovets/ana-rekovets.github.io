Causal Inference · Instrumental Variables · LMU Munich · Mar 2026

Reassessing the Causal Interpretation of TSLS

Building on Blandhol et al. (2022), this paper examines whether standard Two-Stage Least Squares retains a valid Local Average Treatment Effect interpretation once covariates are added — a question about the functional form used to partial out covariates, not just the choice of instrument. Applying Double/Debiased Machine Learning and IPSW estimators to Project STAR (N=11,599 students), the paper finds that TSLS fails the Ramsey RESET diagnostic in every grade cohort, with specification bias accounting for 32.6% of the TSLS estimate — exceeding the very selection bias the instrumental-variables strategy was designed to eliminate.

Early-Life Cash Transfers: A Causal Forest Approach

Structural Estimation · Industrial Organization · NYU Stern · Jun 2026

Hotel Search Demand Estimation & Algorithmic Pricing Simulation

Estimates the causal effect of hotel prices on demand using a 9.9M-row Expedia search dataset via six specifications (OLS, within-session fixed effects, IV/2SLS, DDML with gradient-boosted regressors) and a sequential search structural model (Ursu et al., 2024) estimated by Simulated Maximum Likelihood, isolating price and display-position confounds using randomized-order search sessions. A companion Q-learning simulation with three sellers replicates algorithmic collusion dynamics (Liu et al., 2026), reaching perfect collusion (Δ=1.00) across 50 seeds, and extends the model to noisy competitor-price signals with implications for EU Digital Markets Act price-transparency rules.

Causal Inference · Labor Economics · NYC Citywide Payroll · Aug 2026

Pay Equity in the New York City Municipal Workforce

Using the full FY2024 NYC citywide payroll — 217,402 full-time, full-year salaried employees, with sex imputed from first names via SSA birth records — this paper measures the gender pay gap and decomposes it into the portion operating within jobs versus the portion operating through job allocation. Women earn 5.9% less in raw terms and 7.5% less after adjusting for tenure and agency; conditional on title, the within-job gap is a precise statistical null (-0.08%), while -7.42 points of the adjusted gap runs through job allocation, with women holding titles paying 9.1% less. Two methodological findings are highlighted alongside the estimates: a routine full-time screen that silently excluded a 74.5%-female workforce and doubled the apparent gap, and quantile standard errors that ignored clustering and inflated apparent significance by more than an order of magnitude.
