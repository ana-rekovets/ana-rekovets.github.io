Working Paper

Reassessing the Causal Interpretation of Two-Stage Least Squares

LMU Munich · Stata, R

Using data from a randomized field experiment (11,600+ student observations), I apply Double Machine Learning to detect specification bias in TSLS estimates. The paper empirically demonstrates the failure of the rich covariates condition and quantifies the resulting downward bias, with robustness checks and a fully documented replication repository.

Rent Dynamics & Migration Shocks: Evidence from Russia, 2022

LMU Munich · Sector Study · Stata

Estimates the causal impact of the February and September 2022 forced migration shocks on regional housing markets using administrative data. Quasi-experimental interrupted time series (ITS) analysis reveals statistically significant post-shock upward rent trends with implications for short-run housing affordability.

Interrupted Time Series Housing Economics Migration

Working Paper

Public Policy Impact: Early-Life Cash Transfers — A Causal Forest Approach

LMU Munich · R (grf)

Applies Causal Forests to quasi-experimental data (16,000+ linked historical administrative records) to evaluate social policy impacts on longevity. Estimates an average treatment effect of +1.5% log longevity and maps significant treatment heterogeneity across regional socioeconomic groups, with full robustness analysis and documented replication code.

Causal Forests Heterogeneous Treatment Effects Social Policy

Working Paper

Strategic Policy Analysis: Innovation Subsidies and Firm Productivity

LMU Munich · R (DoubleML, grf)

Estimates average and heterogeneous treatment effects of an R&D subsidy on firm productivity (2,381 firms) using Causal Forests, DDML, DR-TMLE, and Instrumental Forests. Addresses selection on observables, attrition, and unobserved confounding; finds robust ATEs and substantial CATE heterogeneity across firm types.

DDML DR-TMLE R&D Subsidies CATE
2024
Bachelor's Thesis

The Impact of Immigration Shocks on the Housing Market

Higher School of Economics · R

Constructs and manages a monthly 10-year panel dataset combining administrative migration flows and macroeconomic controls. Demonstrates that a 1% increase in migration inflows causes a 1.4% immediate rent increase and a 0.8% lagged increase, with a 3× amplification during the September 2022 shock.

Difference-in-Differences Panel Data Housing Markets Migration

Econometric analysis of a macroeconomic panel (108 countries, 40 years). Applies R-squared decompositions using Shapley and Owen values to assess the relative contributions of socio-cultural and economic drivers of global income inequality.
